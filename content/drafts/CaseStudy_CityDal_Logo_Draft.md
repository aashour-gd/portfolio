## Problem
CityDal helps people find places, events and nearby deals in their city. That is a crowded idea. Every map app has a pin and every deals app has a tag, so a logo that says only "location" disappears next to them. The product also had two audiences with two apps: people looking for places, and businesses listing themselves. The brand had to be one thing in both. And it had to survive the smallest place a brand lives: an app icon on a home screen.

## Hard Decision
**One mark had to carry the name and the idea, so I built it from the name's own letters.**

The documented choice: the mark combines a C, a D and a map pin (C and D from CityDal, the pin from "find a place") into a single shape. It reads as a letter, a pin and a lens at once, which fits the tagline "Reveal the City's Treasured Mysteries". ## What I Built
**The mark and its rules.** Horizontal and vertical logos, a thumbnail for app icons, a mono version, light and dark backgrounds, a construction grid, a safe zone, a minimum size, and a misuse page. The rules exist because the mark has to survive a small header and an app icon.

**The colour system.** Two primaries, Coral Red (#F64440) and Cosmic Cobalt (#3A3383), used in a 60/30/10 split with neutrals. Four supporting colours (Yellow Green, Maya Blue, Beer, Dark Cerulean) are kept small so the primaries stay the brand. CMYK values are in the guidelines for print. [CONFIRM the reason for coral with cobalt.]

**Type.** Inter in Regular, SemiBold and Bold, with a hierarchy for headings, body and buttons. [CONFIRM why Inter: legibility on small screens, licence, Arabic pairing?]

**The brand in the product.** The mark appears on the splash and sign-in screens of both apps, as the thumbnail icon, and as the "Business" lockup in the provider app. Coral drives actions and cobalt anchors headers and primary buttons, which is the 60/30/10 rule applied to UI.
