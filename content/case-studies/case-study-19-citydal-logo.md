# CityDal: a logo that says the name and shows the idea

![The CityDal mark built from a C, a lowercase d and a map pin combining into a single logo mark.](/assets/citydal/citydal-12.png)

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Brand identity and visual system |
| **Timeline** | 2023 — brand guidelines v1.0, July 2023 |
| **Domain** | City discovery, local deals and bookings; Qatar |
| **Deliverable** | Mark, logo variants, colour and type system, brand guidelines, applied to two apps |
| **Scale** | One mark; logo variants (horizontal, vertical, thumbnail, mono, on colour); 10 colours (2 primary, 4 secondary, 4 neutral); Inter in 3 weights; guidelines v1.0, 12 sections |
| **Status** | Shipped in the apps; later discontinued by the client |

## Problem

CityDal helps people find places, events and nearby deals in their city. That is a crowded idea. Every map app has a pin and every deals app has a tag, so a logo that says only "location" disappears next to them. The product also had two audiences with two apps: people looking for places, and businesses listing themselves. The brand had to be one thing in both. And it had to survive the smallest place a brand lives: an app icon on a home screen.

## Role

I owned the brand identity and the visual system in 2023, and I designed both apps on top of it.

## Hard Decision

**One mark had to carry the name and the idea, so I built it from the name's own letters.**

The mark combines a C, a d and a map pin — the C and d from CityDal, the pin from "find a place" — into a single shape. It reads as a letter, a pin and a lens at once, which fits the tagline "Reveal the City's Treasured Mysteries".

![The CityDal logo mark shown in white over green, dark blue, orange and red backgrounds.](/assets/citydal/citydal-10.png)

A second, smaller decision: **two apps, one mark.** The provider app uses the same mark and wordmark with a small "Business" label, so the logo changes with the app a person opens.

## What I Built

**The mark and its rules.** Horizontal and vertical logos, a thumbnail for app icons, a mono version, light and dark backgrounds, a construction grid, a safe zone, a minimum size, and a misuse page. The rules exist because the mark has to survive a small header and an app icon.

![The compressed CityDal thumbnail mark for app icons, shown on coral, cobalt, white and black tiles.](/assets/citydal/citydal-22.png)

![The CityDal logo applied over six backgrounds — brand colours, photography, white and black.](/assets/citydal/citydal-17.png)

**The colour system.** Two primaries, Coral Red (#F64440) and Cosmic Cobalt (#3A3383), used in a 60/30/10 split with neutrals. Four supporting colours — Yellow Green, Maya Blue, Beer and Dark Cerulean — are kept small so the primaries stay the brand. CMYK values are in the guidelines for print.

![The CityDal colour palette: two primary brand colours, four secondary colours and four neutrals, with hex values.](/assets/citydal/citydal-26.png)

**Type.** Inter in Regular, SemiBold and Bold, with a hierarchy for headings, body and buttons.

![CityDal uses Inter in Regular, SemiBold and Bold.](/assets/citydal/citydal-30.png)

**The brand in the product.** The mark appears on the splash and sign-in screens of both apps, as the thumbnail icon, and as the "Business" lockup in the provider app. Coral drives actions and cobalt anchors headers and primary buttons — the 60/30/10 rule applied to the UI.

## Process

| Phase | What exists |
|---|---|
| Frame | Brand purpose and vision |
| Model | Mark Origins: C + d + pin |
| Decide | Mark, palette and type choices recorded in the guidelines |
| Build | Brand Guidelines v1.0 — logo, colour and type; 12 sections |
| Prove | No icon-legibility test or A/B was recorded |
| Ship | Mark shipped in the user and provider apps |

## Result

Interim and honest.

- Brand Guidelines v1.0 delivered in July 2023.
- The mark shipped in both apps and was used for the product's run of about six months before the client discontinued it.

**What is not proven:** that the mark helped people remember or find the product; that it reads better than a plain pin in tests (none ran); recognition or download effects; and how it performs in Arabic contexts (the apps shipped in English).

## Learning

A logo works hardest when it answers the product's question before the screen does. People open a discovery app asking "where?", so a mark that says "a place, and it's called CityDal" earns its spot on the home screen.
